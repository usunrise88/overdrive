# ADR 0043: Progress over time

Status: accepted (release 0.4.1, the data of the companion's Progress screen: service, contracts, Kotlin models and repositories; the screen is the next stage, 2026-11-22).

## Context

The owner wants to see their progress over months with measures other than FTP, for power and for heart rate. `design/companion.md` §4.16, §6.13, §7.9 and §11 specify a pushed **Progress** screen of six cards: **Best power**, **Weight and watts per kilogram**, **Aerobic efficiency**, **Efficiency factor**, **Aerobic drift** (moved from the Power tab) and **Resting heart rate**, each with a verdict in words and a row on Fitness and Power that shows the clearest verdict. The rules of the project hold: the service computes every number and every verdict sentence and the phone only words and draws (`CLAUDE.md`); the LLM never calculates; Strava is write-only (nothing here reads it); the repository is public, so every figure in a test is invented and no health value is ever logged.

The repeat page (ADR 0026) already says "fitter" for one thing: heart rate at the same power over repeated sessions, with a least-squares trend, a steady band, a minimum number of points and a plain caution. This stage generalises that rule to every ride (not only planned steady steps) and to the other measures, and puts it behind one endpoint.

**One table, because it is needed.** A steady stretch is found in the ride's samples, which are megabytes for a long ride; a five-year range would read every record on every request. `ride_progress_stats` keeps, per ride and tied to the record's SHA-256 and a version (as `ride_metrics` and `ride_repeat_stats` do), the stretches and the efficiency factor. It is worked out lazily: a read works out the rides of its range that lack a row, `start()` works out all of them in the background, and a stored ride gets its row at once (`ride-stored`). Everything else is derived from what exists: `ride_metrics` (the power curve at 300, 1200 and 3600 s, the aerobic drift, the FTP of the ride, the workout name), the wellness daily table (resting heart rate, weight), `ftp_history`, the profile. After the first read of a range every later read opens no record (tested with a spy, with 130 rides over five years).

### 2. The rules

Every constant is exported from `contracts/src/progress.ts` and listed in spec §6.8.

**Weeks, months, ranges.** A week is Monday to Sunday and a month is a calendar month, on the rider's local days (`TIMEZONE`). A week or month that the range touches is **never cut**: a "6 months" range that starts on the 25th still shows that month's true best. The service turns each ride's start instant into a local day once, with the same function every other module uses, and all period arithmetic is on `YYYY-MM-DD` strings after that, so a clock change cannot move a ride across a boundary (tested with rides 30 minutes either side of local midnight across both clock changes of `Europe/Belgrade`, where a fixed offset would be wrong by one period). The default range is the last six months; at most five years; a `to` after today is today (the answer says so); the first day cannot be after today.

**Steady stretch** (aerobic efficiency and the efficiency factor): a run of at least **5 minutes**, **after the ride's first 60 s**, with no paused second and a power reading in every second, each second at **40% of the ride's own FTP or more**, whose **30-second rolling means** have a **coefficient of variation under 8%** and none **more than 10% from the stretch's mean**, and a **heart rate (30 to 250 bpm) in at least 90% of its seconds**. These numbers are the proposal of the brief, kept because they have a stated reason each: 5 minutes is the least in which a mean heart rate means anything; 60 s skips the start; 40% of FTP keeps coasting and warm-up out; 8% and 10% are what a steady hold on a trainer does (a ±6 W wave around 200 W passes, blocks of 150 and 250 W do not); 90% coverage is the drift metric's 95% relaxed for short stretches. Found greedily from the start of the ride: a start second is extended while the stretch stays steady; of the starts in the next 30 s the one that reaches furthest is taken (a start a few seconds into the edge of an effort dilutes away and stops short; this finds the clean start), the earliest on a tie; the next search starts after it. A sprint in the middle of a long steady effort ends one stretch and a new one starts after it (the test has 1381 s, a 10 s sprint, 891 s, each worked out by hand). It reads samples, not the plan, so free rides and workouts count alike.

**References** (aerobic efficiency). The **reference power** is **70% of the FTP in force at the range's start, rounded to 5 W**; the **reference heart rate** is **80% of the threshold heart rate (the profile's, else its estimate from the rides), else of the maximum, rounded to 5 bpm**. Both are **fixed for the whole requested range and named in the response** (`referencePower`, `referenceHeartRate`, `ftpAtStart`, `referenceHeartRateBasis`), so an FTP change in the middle of the range never moves them silently. When the range began before the profile had an FTP, the earliest FTP known is used; with none, or with no heart rate known, the reference is `null` and the trend says why in words. 70% of FTP is an easy-aerobic effort the owner rides often, 80% of the threshold heart rate is the same zone from the other side; the two are separate figures because a rider who got fitter shows it as a lower heart rate at the power *or* a higher power at the heart rate.

**How near stretches are used: not at all.** A week's **heart rate at the reference power** is the **median of the mean heart rates of its stretches whose mean power is within 5% of the reference power**; its **power at the reference heart rate** is the median of the mean powers of its stretches whose mean heart rate is within 3 bpm of the reference. A week with no such stretch has no figure (a gap, never a zero). The alternative, adjusting each stretch to the reference with a linear heart-rate-on-power slope limited to ±5%, needs a slope that is itself an estimate from noisy data; the simpler honest rule is to use only what is at the reference and to say how many stretches and seconds stand behind each figure (`atPower`, `atHeartRate`). The median, not the mean, because one stretch with a spiking heart-rate strap should not move a week.

**Efficiency factor.** Normalised power over average heart rate (Friel's efficiency factor), of the **steady parts of a ride taken together** (its stretches one after the other), for a ride whose steady parts add up to **at least 20 minutes**; one value per ride (three decimals), with `rolling5`, the mean of the last five rides from the fifth on. Taking it over the steady parts and not over the whole ride is what makes it comparable between a free ride and a 2×20 workout; the cost is that a ride with no steady 20 minutes has none.

**Aerobic drift.** The per-ride figure of spec §6.3 as `ride_metrics` already holds it (rides of 40 minutes or more that are steady enough, 95% coverage, variability index at most 1.05), not recomputed; with `rolling5`.

**Best power.** The best **300, 1200 and 3600 s** mean-maximal power of each calendar month from the stored power curve, **tablet rides only** (the curve is the tablet's: outdoor rides and the watch's workouts never feed it, spec §11.8); the later ride wins a tie. Watts per kilogram uses **the 7-day average weight at the end of the best ride's day, else the month's mean weight, else none**, and each entry carries the weight it used. The current month is `partial` and **not in the trend**: a month so far is lower than a month, and would draw a false decline at the end of every month.

**Resting heart rate and weight.** The mean of the week's nightly resting heart rate, **from 3 nights**; the mean of the week's daily weights, **from 2 weighings**; `nights` and `weighings` are always counted. `wattsPerKg` is the **FTP in force at the end of the week** (today for the current week) over the weekly mean weight. Weight is reported (`weightChange`: first, last, difference) and **never judged**; the trend of the weight card is of watts per kilogram. With no FTP in force in a week (the history starts later than the week) that week has no watts per kilogram, and the weight is still reported.

**Trend and verdict.** A least-squares line over the points that have a figure, against their place in time: the week or month number (so a missing week is a gap and not a squeeze: the test has weeks 0, 1, 3, 4 and a change of −4, not −3) or, for the two ride measures, the ride's position. The fitted change is the slope times the span; smaller than the **steady band** in size it is `steady`, else `improving` or `worsening` by which way is better; **below the minimum number of points the direction is `unknown`** (two points always make a line, one comparison is not a trend). Minimums and bands are the design's table: best power 3 months per duration and 2% of the first figure; aerobic efficiency 4 weeks, 2 bpm and 4 W; efficiency factor 5 rides, 0.02; drift 5 rides, 1 point; resting heart rate 4 weeks, 1 bpm; watts per kilogram 4 weeks, 0.05 W/kg. Best power's **overall** verdict is improving when at least two of its three durations improve and none worsens, worsening the reverse, unknown below two durations with a verdict, else steady. **The words are the service's** (`verdictText`): "fitter" is said only for heart rate against power (aerobic efficiency and the efficiency factor), "better" for the other measures when they improve, **no judging word when a trend worsens** ("Heart rate at the same power is trending up"), "Not enough data yet" when unknown, with a `reason` that counts what there is ("3 of 4 weeks have a figure. Ride steady for 5 minutes or more to add one."). Heart-rate measures carry the standing `note` about heat, sleep and caffeine. The contract's validator and the Kotlin model both recompute the direction from `change`, `steadyBand` and `betterWhen`, so a body whose sentence and numbers disagree is refused.

**Headline.** One verdict for the Fitness and Power rows. Among the measures with a **non-steady** verdict, the one whose change is **furthest outside its band** (`|change| / steadyBand`; best power's score is the mean over its lines), the page's order (aerobic efficiency, best power, efficiency factor, resting heart rate, drift, weight) deciding a tie; with none, the first **steady** verdict in that order; with no verdict anywhere, `null` (the row says "How your power and heart rate change over the months"). A verdict that is clearly outside noise is the most informative; a steady one is shown only when nothing moves.

### 3. What the design said and the data says (differences)

- **The words are in both places.** The design says the service sends the direction and the phone owns the words; the brief asks for `verdictText` written by code (and the agent needs the sentences). Both are sent; the strings match the design's table (best power also has one per duration: "Best 20-minute power is trending up: better"); a screen may use either, and must not compute a verdict.
- **Reference power rounds to 5 W** (design: 10 W) and **the steady stretch skips the first 60 s** (design: the first 10 minutes), as the brief says: a rule that needs 10 minutes of warm-up would leave out most free rides of an hour; the cost is that the first minutes of a stretch include the heart rate still settling (60 s is also what the repeat page leaves out).
- **Efficiency factor is of the steady parts of a ride of 20 minutes or more**, not "per steady ride" of a rule the design leaves open.
- **Best power:** each duration of a month is `null` or `{ watts, wattsPerKg, weightKg, date, rideId, rideName }`, with the weight per entry (the design had one `weightKg` for the block and `watts: null` entries); every month of the range is listed (a gap is a month with `null`s).
- **Aerobic efficiency:** a week has `atPower` and `atHeartRate` `{ stretches, seconds }` (the design had one pair, but the two figures use different stretches); the two trends are `trend` (heart rate at the reference power, the card's verdict) and `powerTrend`; `ftpAtStart` and `referenceHeartRateBasis` are added so the screen can say where the references come from.
- **Trend fields added to the design's list:** `unit`, `difference` (latest minus from, so the screen does not subtract), `verdictText`, `reason`, `note`; `since` is the day of `from`; for the two ride measures `from` and `latest` are the means of the first and last five rides ("1.39 over the last 5 steady rides, 1.32 in June"); `change` is the fitted change, which can differ from `difference` by noise, and the verdict follows `change`.
- **`rolling5` starts at the fifth ride** (a mean of fewer would look like a five-ride mean).
- **Errors:** a bad range is `400 invalid_range` naming the parameter, like `GET /v1/metrics` (the brief said `invalid_json`, which in this API is for a body that is not JSON; no new code, the closed list is unchanged).
- **The headline has a `verdictText`** besides `measure` and `direction`.
- **The weight card's "not enough" reason** counts weeks with two weighings *and* an FTP ("2 of 4 weeks have 2 weighings and an FTP."), because watts per kilogram needs both.

### 4. Kotlin and what the screen calls

Strictly decoded models (`ProgressApiJson.decode`: unknown keys, numbers in quotes, missing keys and every consistency rule are an `IllegalArgumentException`; `ProgressContractTest` runs the five valid and 85 invalid fixtures, and the valid ones are written back equal), `ProgressRange` (`SIX_MONTHS`, `ONE_YEAR`, `ALL_TIME` = five years; `from(today)`), `ProgressRepository` (one `RemoteResource` per range, cached under `progress-6m`, `progress-1y`, `progress-all`, shown at once and offline with the cause when old, the same shape as the metrics resources), and the companion container wiring. **The UI stage calls exactly:**

- `container.progressFor(range: ProgressRange): RemoteResource<ProgressResponse>`: read `.state` (a `ResourceState`: `value`, `fetchedAtMillis` for "Updated 07:40", `problem` for the StatusLine, `isLoading`, `isStale`). The caches of all three ranges are read at start.
- `container.screenOpened(RefreshTarget.Progress(range))` when the screen opens or the range changes (at most one request a minute per range) and `container.refresh(RefreshTarget.Progress(range))` for pull-to-refresh (always asks, retries a refused token once).
- The rows of **Fitness and Power** read `container.progressFor(ProgressRange.DEFAULT).state.value?.value?.headline` (`measure`, `direction`, `verdictText`; null shows the neutral sentence). Opening Fitness or Power, and new watch data reaching the service, already refresh the default range.
- **Sync now** (`container.syncNow()`) asks all three ranges.
- The remembered range is the screen's: `CompanionSettings` has no `progressRange` yet (add it beside `fitnessRange` and `powerRange`).
- Models: `app.overdrive.core.progress.ProgressResponse` and its parts; the phone computes no trend and no verdict.

## Limits and what only real data settles

- **One rider; power measures read tablet rides only.** Outdoor rides have no power, and the watch's workouts feed no curve.
- **Heart rate is noisy:** heat, sleep, caffeine, hydration, the strap. A stretch of 5 minutes is short for heart rate to settle at a new power (the first 60 s are left out, not the 3 to 4 minutes it can take); the medians and the minimums are there for that, and the trend says so in its `note`. The efficiency factor also depends on intensity: a threshold session and an endurance ride have different values, so the trend is only meaningful across similar riding.
- **Thresholds are first guesses to tune on real rides:** 8% and 10% of steadiness, 90% coverage, 70% of FTP and 80% of the threshold heart rate, ±5% and ±3 bpm, the bands, the minimums. The owner's own rides will say whether a typical week has stretches at the reference (a rider who never rides at 70% of FTP for 5 steady minutes gets no figure, and the card says so).
- **The first read of years of rides is slow** (every record is opened once); it is done in the background at start and the answer waits for the rides of its own range only.
- **FTP before the history.** The FTP history starts when the profile first had an FTP (or the metrics stage began recording); weeks before it have no watts per kilogram, and a range that starts earlier takes the earliest FTP known for the references.
- **Not run on the owner's data:** all tests use invented numbers on synthetic rides (hand-worked goldens in `server/test/progress-steady.test.ts`, `progress-compute.test.ts`, `progress-api.test.ts`; the API tests also run on a real PostgreSQL 18).

## Consequences

- The Progress screen can be built from `ProgressResponse` alone; it adds no rule and computes nothing. Adding a measure is a contract change first (schema, fixtures, validators, Kotlin) and then the service.
- Changing a rule of the steady stretch is a new `STRETCH_VERSION` in `server/src/progress/steady.ts`: the rows are worked out again on the next read.
- Migration `0013` adds one table; nothing existing changed.
